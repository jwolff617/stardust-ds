# Job Match DS

Jobs, gigs, people looking for work, and free training, in Seattle. Built on Posts. The point is getting someone from "I need work" to a real conversation with a real employer, without the ghosting, junk listings, and scams that fill the big boards.

## What the incumbents do

**Indeed.** Enormous. Easy apply. Employers can post for free.
- Bad: full of reposted, expired, and fake ("ghost") jobs that companies leave up to look like they're growing. Most applicants never hear back. Pay is often missing or a range so wide it means nothing. Ads and paid "sponsored" jobs float to the top. Applying wants a resume and an account, which is hard on a phone. Scam jobs (reshipping, fake checks, "pay for your training") get through.

**Idealist.** Nonprofit and mission jobs, volunteer roles, and a real culture of posting pay. Good filters for cause and remote.
- Bad: national, so local small orgs get lost. Few hourly and entry-level jobs. Almost nothing for someone rebuilding after jail, treatment, or homelessness.

**Craigslist gigs and jobs.** Fast, local, and the place people actually find day labor, moving help, and cash gigs.
- Bad: no names, constant scams (fake checks, "send me your info"), and in Seattle it charges employers for job posts. Little trust either way.

**LinkedIn.** Reputation and networks.
- Bad: built for office careers. Irrelevant to most hourly work and to people without a polished history.

**WorkSource and the nonprofits (Recovery Cafe, FareStart, Pioneer Human Services, the Y).** Real people helping real people into work, often with training and second-chance employers.
- Bad: each has its own list, flyer, or email, so a participant has to know which door to knock on.

## What Stardust does differently

1. **Every job and gig says what it pays.** Pay is required. Washington already requires a pay range from employers with 15 or more people; on Stardust everyone does it.
2. **Fair chance, said out loud.** An employer can mark a job "People with records are welcome," and it shows as a green badge and a filter. Seattle's Fair Chance rules already keep job ads from shutting out people with records; Job Match turns that into a welcome.
3. **No experience needed, also a filter.** So a first job or a fresh start is easy to find.
4. **A promise to answer.** Each job says how soon it answers everyone who writes: within 3 days or within a week. When it's filled, Mark filled sends one kind note to every applicant. Nobody is left wondering.
5. **Apply by message, from any phone.** No resume, no account on another site. A short "Use a starter" fills a few lines (when you can work, what you've done) you can change. Someone on a library computer can apply in two minutes.
6. **Looking posts.** People post what work they want and when they're free. Employers and nonprofits can find them. A Looking post matches jobs, gigs, and trainings that share its labels, and those show as "Matches your Looking post."
7. **Training as its own kind.** Free trainings with a stipend, food handler cards, flagger certification, forklift: posted by the nonprofits that run them, with what you leave with.
8. **Posts expire fast.** Jobs and Looking last 30 days, gigs 7, trainings 45. No ghost jobs.
9. **Organizations post as themselves.** The Y, Recovery Cafe, and other verified organizations carry a badge.
10. **Fair hiring wording check.** Job posts are read for wording that turns people away (young, recent grads only, no felons, native English). The rule shows in plain words before it posts.
11. **No ads, no sponsored jobs, no fee to post.** Nobody pays to be on top.

## Kinds and fields

| Kind | Money line | Its own fields | Lasts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job | Pay (required) | Hours (full time, part time, weekends only, seasonal), Schedule, Fair chance (people with records are welcome), Experience (none needed, some, experienced), How to apply (message here, in person, phone, online), Where or how (address, number, or link), We answer everyone (within 3 days, within a week) | 30 days |
| Gig | Pay (required) | When, About how long, What to bring | 7 days |
| Looking | none | Work I'm looking for, When I can work, What I've done | 30 days |
| Training | Cost (free, stipend) | When, How long, You leave with (a card, a certificate), Who it's for | 45 days |

Every post has a title, details, neighborhood, up to 10 labels, and can be posted as an organization. No photos on Job Match; a job is words.

**Labels suggested:** kitchen, dishwasher, cleaning, janitorial, warehouse, retail, childcare, after-school, driving, delivery, construction, landscaping, moving, events, security, office, healthcare, outreach, peer-support, remote, bus-line.

**Matches.** Looking matches Job, Gig, and Training. Job, Gig, and Training match Looking. A match needs a shared label.

## The words

| Term | Part | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Job | more | Steady paid work. Says what it pays, the hours, and how to apply. |
| Gig | more | Paid work for a day or a few: moving, an event, a yard. Says what it pays. |
| Looking | more | A spark looking for work: what kind, and when they can work. |
| Training | more | A class or program that leads to work, with what you leave with. |
| Pay | noun | What the work pays, in dollars, said up front. Every job and gig on Job Match says it. |
| Fair Chance | noun | A welcome to people with records. A fair chance employer looks at the person in front of them, not only their past. |
| Apply | verb | Send a short message to the spark or organization that posted the work. When you can work and what you've done is enough. |
| Filled | more | The job has someone. Everyone who wrote hears so. |

## Safety and operations

**Scams Job Match watches for in messages.** Fake checks and "send back the difference," reshipping packages, paying for training or equipment to start, gift cards, and asking for a Social Security number or bank details in a message. Each gets a plain warning under the message. The board's safety notes say the same.

**Staying safe (on the board):**
- A real job never asks you to pay to start, buy equipment, or reship packages.
- Don't send your Social Security number or bank details in a message. Paperwork comes after you're hired, in person.
- Meet for an interview at the workplace or a public place in daylight.
- If something feels off, Report it. A steward will look.

**Fair hiring.** The wording check covers age, sex, records, language and citizenship, disability, religion, and parents. It warns and explains; it's guidance, not legal advice. "Unfair" reports go to stewards.

**Pay is required** on Job and Gig, in the form and in the API, so Sage AI can't skip it either.

**Expiry.** Short on purpose. A job someone forgot to close drops off on day 30.

**Who reviews.** Stewards, same as every board. A Recovery Cafe or SHARE staff member as a steward of Job Match makes sense first, since they know the scams that target their people.

**Looking posts and privacy.** Every signed-in spark can read a Looking post. The form says so, suggests a first name and neighborhood only, and no phone number unless you want calls.

## Sage AI

Noun **Job Post** (in Job Match):
- Add, Edit, Find (kind, query, label, mine), Archive (mark filled, notes go out), Restore, Delete, Act Renew.
- Adds run the fair-hiring check strictly and need pay on Job and Gig.
- Sage AI can help someone write a Looking post: "I'm looking for kitchen work, mornings, I've washed dishes for two years."

## Real people, walked through

**James, Recovery Cafe member, looking for a dishwasher job.** He's on the Cafe computer. He adds Job Match, taps Fair chance and No experience, and the label dishwasher. Two jobs show, both with pay up top. One is from a verified organization, a FareStart-style kitchen. He taps Apply, uses the starter ("I can work mornings. I've washed dishes for two years."), and sends. He also posts Looking: "Dishwasher or prep cook, mornings," labels dishwasher, kitchen. Next week a new kitchen job with the dishwasher label shows "Matches your Looking post." The first job hits its 3-day promise: the employer answers, he goes in. When it's filled, the other applicants get a kind note.
- Check: Fair chance and No experience are filters he can tap; pay is on every card; apply is a message; the Looking match works by label. Works.

**Rosa, SHARE participant, library computer and a phone.** She wants day work. She taps Gig. A moving gig in Lake City pays $25/hr for about 4 hours, Saturday. She messages from the library; the next afternoon she checks on her phone and "Your turn (1)" shows the answer. Later a "gig" message says she'll be paid by a check for more than the job and should send back the rest. The yellow Careful note shows. She reports the conversation, which blocks him too.
- Check: Your turn works across devices. The overpayment scam line fires. Works.

**Priya, YMCA after-school staff, posting a part-time opening.** The Y is an organization on Stardust and she's a member, so the form shows "Post as: YMCA of Greater Seattle." She posts a Job: "After-school program leader, Hazel Valley." Pay: $21 to $23/hr. Hours: Part time. Schedule: weekdays 2:15 to 6. Experience: Some. We answer everyone: within a week. She first wrote "energetic young person"; the form explained the age rule and she changed it to "lots of energy and happy outside with kids." Eight people write. She answers each, hires one, and taps Mark filled; seven people get the note.
- Check: organization badge shows verified; the hiring check caught "young." Works.

**Sam, a Wallingford neighbor.** Needs help moving a couch Saturday. Posts a Gig, $60 for about an hour. Gets three replies in an evening, picks one, closes it.

## How it grows

1. **Recovery Cafe.** Its staff post trainings and the second-chance jobs they already know about. Members post Looking. Needs: Recovery Cafe as a verified organization, one staff member as a steward.
2. **The Y.** Part-time program openings each season. Needs: the Y as an organization, Priya or a site lead as a member who can post as the Y.
3. **SHARE.** Day work and gigs for participants. Needs: a SHARE steward who knows the scams.
4. **Employers who say fair chance.** One by one, through the nonprofits. A verified badge comes from Sage talking to a real person there.

**What an organization's page shows (later).** Its name, verified badge, neighborhood, a line about it, and its open posts on each board. For now, the badge on each post and the about line are what show.
