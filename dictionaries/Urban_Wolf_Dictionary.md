## Design Principles

**Walkable** — A neighborhood where every need — food, work, play, rest — is within a 1-5 minute walk on a trail. Not a luxury feature. A human right. The camp model applied to cities. When everything is walkable, everything becomes possible.

**Courtyard Block** — A residential block built around a shared courtyard. Not a backyard — a shared front yard. The place where neighbors become community. Children play. Adults talk. Density without isolation.

**Density** — The proximity of people that makes love possible. Not overcrowding — the right scale of closeness. Density is where lovers find each other. Where neighbors know each other. Where the economy of care becomes possible.

**Secret Trail** — The path through a neighborhood that is not on the map. Every great community has them. Secret trails are evidence that people have claimed the space as their own.

**Freedom School** — A school built around the camp model. Nature-close. Play-rich. Walking everywhere. Attendance redefined not as presence in a building but as engagement with learning. Built for all kinds of minds. Especially built for the maniac.

**Connected Paths** — The urban design principle that everything is built around. Before the buildings, the zoning, the transit lines — build the paths that connect people to each other. The trail is the city's nervous system. Everything else is organs.

## Infrastructure Ideas

**Neural Transit Network** — A transit system that learns the movement patterns of a community and adapts in real time. Not fixed routes — responsive routes. Transit that follows people rather than forcing people to follow transit.

**Micro-Transit** — Small vehicles, small routes, human scale. The van that serves a neighborhood, not the bus that serves a corridor. Transit designed for the last mile and the first connection.

**Micro-Entrepreneurship** — Small businesses at the neighborhood scale. Not franchise chains — people selling what they make, know, or love to the people around them. The economic engine of the courtyard community.

**Double Helix Market** — A market designed in a double helix pattern so that every vendor has equal access to foot traffic. No prime spots. No dead corners. Democratic market design.

**Build the Trails** — The first act of any community improvement. Before the buildings, before the plans — build the trails. Connect the people. Give them paths to find each other. Everything else follows.

**Jungle Gym Design** — Public play structures designed not just for children but for all ages. Play is not age-limited. A neighborhood with good jungle gyms is a neighborhood where people stop and meet each other.

**Roundabout** — A traffic design that slows cars, keeps people moving, and eliminates the stop-and-wait culture of the intersection. The roundabout is a metaphor for the city Sage Peace wants to build: everyone keeps moving, no one has absolute right of way, the flow is continuous.
